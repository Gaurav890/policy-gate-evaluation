\begin{abstract}
Evaluations of policy gates for tool-using language-model agents often
report warning rates, block rates or task success, but these measure
different properties. We present an evaluation method that links each
decision to its policy target, visible evidence, native execution
consequence and subsequent task outcome. Across simulated retail and
airline environments, the same saved warnings yield alert rates of
64.7\% or 4.1\% on successful conversations depending on whether the
evaluation group also requires interaction-rule compliance. In 1,152
live conversations, two example gate designs block 161 writes: 48 would
produce violations of the checked rules that would change the database,
whereas 26 would already be rejected by the native tools. Relative to
ungated runs, gating reduces observed executed violations by about ten
percentage points and reference-task completion by six to nine points,
accounting for unresolved outcomes. All ungated violations come from one
of three actor configurations. Matched completion losses sometimes occur
without a block, and some successful endpoints omit a required
confirmation exchange. Linked records, native replay and executable
checks let evaluators distinguish detection, added protection and task
outcomes, while preserving missing observations and unresolved policy
judgments.
\end{abstract}
