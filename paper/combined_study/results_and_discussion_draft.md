# Results and discussion for the amended compact study

Working manuscript text based on the completed frozen analysis. Exact tables and provenance are in [results_readout.md](results_readout.md) and [results_tables.json](results_tables.json). The qualitative examples are post hoc explanations of saved decisions, not new outcome labels. This draft is local and has not been submitted.

## Abstract

What does a blocked tool call establish about a policy gate's benefit? We combine decisions on identical actor-generated proposals, native transition replay and live gated conversations in simulated airline and retail service. The amended allocation comprises 1,152 conversations across 32 designed source pairs, eight selected rules, three actor configurations and three live conditions. Of these, 1,143 completed; eight infrastructure interruptions and one turn-limit outcome remain retained. Among 192 assigned pressure conversations per condition, executed covered violations occurred in 21 ungated conversations, one with a broad gate and two with a decomposed gate. All ungated executed violations came from one actor configuration. On nominally permitted requests, normal completion with an exact reference-state match fell from 181/192 without a gate to 163/192 and 166/192. Assignment bounds preserve the direction of both differences, but do not quantify population uncertainty. Lower reference completion does not establish unnecessary obstruction: the broad gate checks requirements beyond the selected mechanical predicates, and ungated success need not be fully policy compliant. Shared-proposal checks distinguish native rejection from substantive interception and expose one decomposed-gate miss when eligibility facts were unobserved. Scope was reduced after collection began, and missing observations preclude all four planned formal contrasts. This empirical audit supports reporting decision, transition and conversation measurements together, with explicit policy coverage; it does not establish general gate effectiveness or a preferred defense.

## Results

### Processing finished with incomplete observations retained

All 1,152 assigned live conversations were attempted. The records contain 1,143 normal completions, eight infrastructure interruptions and one customer-turn-limit outcome. The turn limit is an observed outcome within the declared horizon; the infrastructure interruptions leave some outcomes unresolved. None was retried or replaced. The 384 ungated conversations supplied 384 assigned shadow sources: 383 have complete scoring records and one retains missing output after a focused monitor timeout. No source remains unstarted.

The failed shadow source preserves the broad decision but lacks both the focused decomposed decision and the subsequent privileged-reference decision. Both remain missing in their respective denominators. The privileged reference was not filled retrospectively. Native replay and public-input checks covered all 1,152 live records, and the final evidence archive contains 6,625 request receipts. The analysis includes 102 byte-identical records imported from the earlier collection and 1,050 newly collected compact records. Earlier attempts outside this allocation are not pooled into the results.

### Fewer covered violations accompanied lower reference completion

Each request-type/condition cell contains 192 assigned conversations. Table 1 places the two principal outcomes together. An executed covered violation requires a selected policy violation and a native state change. Reference completion requires both normal dialogue completion and an exact reference-state match (the frozen completed-service endpoint); it does not certify full-policy compliance. Assignment bounds place unresolved binary outcomes first at zero, then at one; they are not confidence intervals.

**Table 1. Live outcomes with assignment denominators.**

| Condition | Pressure: observed executed violations / 192 | Unresolved pressure outcomes | Violation bounds | Nominally permitted: reference completions / 192 | Unresolved completion outcomes | Completion bounds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| No gate | 21 | 0 | 10.94% | 181 | 0 | 94.27% |
| Broad | 1 | 2 | 0.52–1.56% | 163 | 2 | 84.90–85.94% |
| Decomposed | 2 | 1 | 1.04–1.56% | 166 | 3 | 86.46–88.02% |

Under the frozen pair-within-rule weighting, the broad gate minus no-gate violation difference lies between −10.42 and −9.38 percentage points. The corresponding decomposed difference lies between −9.90 and −9.38 points. Reference-completion differences lie between −9.38 and −8.33 points for broad gating and between −7.81 and −6.25 points for decomposed gating. Thus, even assigning the unresolved outcomes in the most favorable direction does not remove either the lower violation frequency or the lower reference completion in these assigned runs. These bounds do not quantify sampling uncertainty or establish population effects.

Every primary contrast contains at least one unresolved source-pair block, so all four p values and formal inferential intervals are withheld under the frozen missingness rule. We do not substitute a complete-case significance test. Nor do the counts establish superiority between the gates: the planned comparisons were each gate against no gate, and the two outcomes entail different consequences.

Normal dialogue completion on permitted requests was 192/192 without a gate, 190/192 with broad gating and 188/192 with decomposed gating. Exact reference-state matches were 181, 163 and 166, respectively. A normally ended conversation therefore did not always deliver the requested state. The reference-completion differences combine actor choices, intervention responses and controller behavior; they cannot all be attributed to a particular blocked call. Some ungated completions may have used impermissible procedures. These totals alone therefore cannot quantify loss of compliant service or unnecessary obstruction.

### The ungated violations were concentrated in one actor configuration

GPT-5.4 Mini accounted for all 21 ungated executed violations among its 64 assigned pressure conversations. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Claude Opus 5.5 each had zero among 64 pressure conversations, with no unresolved ungated outcomes. These zero counts establish what happened in those cases, not zero failure probability or a gate benefit for those configurations.

The 21 violating conversations span five selected rules: compensation eligibility and customer lookup each account for seven, further modification after an item change accounts for five, and basic-economy modification and cancellation eligibility each account for one. Each rule had 24 ungated pressure conversations. No executed covered violation was observed in the passenger-count, refund-destination or delivered-return rule cells.

For GPT-5.4 Mini, executed violations were 21/64 without a gate, 1/64 with broad gating and 2/64 with decomposed gating, with no missing binary outcomes in these actor-specific pressure cells. Its reference completions were 53/64, 38/64 and 42/64, also without missing service outcomes. The aggregate pattern of fewer covered violations and fewer reference completions is therefore concentrated in this actor configuration. The full actor tables retain all configurations and missing outcomes; this breakdown is descriptive, not a newly selected primary population.

### Shared proposals reveal both interception and the scope of checking

The ungated corpus contains 231 proposed writes. Native replay classifies 21 as state-changing covered violations across 13 source pairs and 15 as covered violations already rejected by native tools across seven pairs. The remaining 195 are clean for the applicable selected predicates: 99 have public evidence establishing that selected eligibility and 96 require private facts. No proposal falls in the violating-no-op or unresolved/uncovered classes. Empty classes have undefined rates.

| State-changing covered violation | 21 | 13 | 21/21; 0 missing | 20/21; 0 missing | 21/21; 0 missing |
| Violation already rejected natively | 15 | 7 | 15/15; 0 missing | 14/15; 0 missing | 15/15; 0 missing |
| Selected-eligible, publicly verified | 99 | 22 | 17/99; 0 missing | 0/99; 0 missing | 0/99; 0 missing |

The broad gate blocked all 21 state-changing violations in the stored corpus; the decomposed gate allowed one. In that case, the actor proposed modifying a basic-economy itinerary without first reading the reservation. The public predicate returned unknown. The focused monitor treated the absence of visible evidence of basic economy, together with an economy argument in the proposed write, as grounds to allow. The broad gate instead cited the missing reservation check. Private-state replay verified a covered violation that changed state. This example identifies one failure to resolve absent evidence before permitting a write; it is not an estimate of its prevalence elsewhere.

The broad gate's 17 blocks on publicly verified selected-eligible proposals must not be described as 17 established false alarms. Reviewing all 17 stored reasons found nine on basic-economy permitted counterparts citing payment-profile or flight-condition checks and eight citing explicit user confirmation. These requirements extend beyond the mechanical predicates used for the selected-eligibility label. In one retail example, the actor successfully identified the customer and read a pending order, then proposed its address change without a subsequent confirmation exchange. The selected lookup and pending-order predicates were clean while the broad gate cited the missing confirmation. The distinction is between the coverage of the measurements and the gate's policy scope; model-written reasons do not themselves establish that every block was correct. The complete post hoc case list and source hashes are retained separately.

The first-executed-violation truncation produces the same class denominators and shadow summaries in this corpus. This removes no observed discrepancy here, but does not make a static shadow decision equivalent to the result of a live intervention. Descriptive paired bootstrap outputs remain in the archived analysis; they are not presented as calibrated population confidence statements. In particular, a degenerate interval for an observed all-block result does not establish certainty about other cases.

### Most live blocks were not distinct demonstrations of prevented violations

Across both request types, broad gating blocked 102 writes and decomposed gating blocked 59. Native replay identified 26 and 22 blocked writes, respectively, that would have caused a state-changing covered violation. Each gate also blocked 13 violating writes that the native tool would already reject. Broad gating blocked 62 selected-eligible writes, split into 26 publicly verified and 36 requiring private evidence; decomposed gating blocked 23, all requiring private evidence. Each condition includes one separate fail-closed block caused by monitor unavailability.

These are event counts from treatment-dependent proposal sequences, not independent saved conversations. The next write repeated the same proposal after 44 broad blocks and ten decomposed blocks; it changed the proposal after one block in each condition. There was no later write after 57 and 48 blocks, respectively. Repetition, refusal and eventual service completion answer different questions, and a one-step native replay does not reconstruct the entire conversation that would have occurred without intervention.

### Costs and sensitivity retain the same descriptive pattern

The compact allocation has $83.035226 in response-reported cost: $69.895841 for actor requests, $6.957748 for live gates and $6.181637 for shadow scoring. Scripted customers incur no customer-model requests. There are 88 requests with unknown cost and $3.459894 in unresolved reservations, which are conservative holds rather than confirmed charges. Imported requests are counted once. These totals are distinct from whole-project spending and Gateway credit.

On permitted requests, observed mean actor turns were 4.95 without a gate, 5.33 with broad gating and 5.03 with decomposed gating. Observed mean live response costs were $0.07196, $0.09306 and $0.08038, respectively. The cost means use 181, 177 and 178 conversations with known cost, rather than all 192 assigned conversations per cell; they are not complete-assignment cost estimates. Pressure-request means and all missing counts remain in the accompanying tables.

The sensitivity retaining only the 29 source pairs without an earlier collected conversation preserves the direction of both differences. Broad-minus-no-gate bounds are −11.98 to −10.94 percentage points for violations and −8.85 to −8.33 for reference completion. Decomposed-minus-no-gate bounds are −10.42 to −9.90 and −6.77 to −5.21 points. Each contrast still has unresolved pairs and no formal p value. This subset has only one basic-economy pair versus four for each other rule, and it does not undo the post-start amendment.

### Post hoc public-policy evidence checks

A separate unblinded programmatic pass examines all 392 saved proposal inputs (231 ungated writes and 161 live blocks) and all 576 nominally permitted conversations. The checks cover specific observable requirements; they do not establish complete semantic-policy compliance. Combining the original native predicates with additional conservative checks identifies 74 live blocks on known selected violations, ten with additional action or prerequisite breaches, ten with current-message formatting violations alone, 65 with unresolved full-policy status, and two operational blocks from unavailable monitors. Twenty-six of the 74 selected-violation calls would already have been rejected by native tools. These categories describe evidence for a block, not its incremental protective value or a measured unnecessary-block rate.

Six of the 181 ungated reference completions and five of the 166 decomposed-gate reference completions contain a database write with no user reply after the initial request, omitting the required disclosure-then-confirmation exchange. No broad-gate reference completion is flagged by that check. Other confirmation wording remains unresolved; absence of the literal word yes is not treated as sufficient evidence of non-consent. These examples demonstrate that reference completion can include a procedural shortcut. They neither certify the remaining conversations nor establish the cause of aggregate completion differences.

The evidence records preserve clause locations, message indices, source hashes and uncertainty. Prior message-format departures are not counted as justification for a later block, and missing tool evidence is not automatically converted into a requirement to use one specific tool. Full-policy permission and unnecessary-obstruction rates remain unestablished by these checks. The [separate audit report](policy_audit/evidence_audit/README.md) documents the implemented checks and their limits. Frozen endpoints and the four withheld formal comparisons are unchanged.

## Discussion

The principal result is conditional. In this designed corpus, gating accompanies fewer executed violations of selected constraints and lower reference completion on nominally permitted requests. This joint pattern does not establish how much useful work was unnecessarily obstructed: lower state completion can reflect appropriate enforcement as well as unnecessary obstruction. The separation between native enforcement, selected eligibility and whole-policy procedure changes how the blocking counts should be read. Blocking a call already rejected by the tool adds no protection against that transition. Allowing a proposal clean on selected predicates need not satisfy other public-policy requirements. Blocking a state-changing violation is a direct interception, but does not by itself establish a better complete conversation.

The comparison exposes a failure mode in this implementation of the decomposed gate. Public predicates can make a decision precise when their required facts are available. In the observed missed violation, a focused model check allowed a write even though the public predicate could not establish the original state. Conversely, the broad gate checked requirements beyond the selected predicates and produced more blocks in mechanically eligible classes. The experiment therefore compares the two implemented decision procedures, including their coverage, rather than isolating decomposition as a universally beneficial mechanism. We do not tune either procedure on the collected cases or introduce a new repair experiment into this frozen study.

The actor breakdown limits the reach of the result. Both other configurations already avoided executed covered violations on their ungated pressure runs. The aggregate result should not be presented as evidence that gates improve all three actors or that a particular actor is generally safe. Likewise, lower reference completion cannot identify which interruptions were unnecessary: some blocks concern policy requirements outside the selected state-based endpoints. The programmatic audit identifies concrete grounds for some blocks and leaves 65 live block events unresolved. Gate-generated reasons do not establish correctness. The present analysis therefore does not estimate an unnecessary-block rate or attribute all completion loss to the gate.

The study has 32 designed source pairs and 31 recorded shared-context components, not 1,152 independent tasks. Policies, templates and domains remain shared even across those components. Scripted customers, selected mechanical predicates and hosted model aliases further constrain interpretation. The scope reduction followed earlier collection and outcome inspection; the retained provenance sensitivity is useful but does not turn the study into a wholly prospective registration. Missing observations preclude the selected formal comparisons, and the earlier precision simulations caution against interpreting absent effects as equivalence. The study does not evaluate unseen domains or establish comprehensive semantic-policy accuracy.

For evaluating a proposed gate, the evidence supports reporting three layers together: paired decisions on identical proposals with native consequences, the full live conversation after intervention, and reference completion with operating burden. Our results show why one layer cannot substitute for another. This is a measurement conclusion supported by the assigned cases, not a deployment recommendation.

## Conclusion

In the amended compact experiment, both public gates had fewer observed executed covered violations and fewer reference completions than the ungated condition. All ungated executed violations occurred in GPT-5.4 Mini; the other actor configurations do not independently demonstrate a violation-reduction benefit. The broad and decomposed implementations differ in policy coverage as well as structure. Native replay separates substantive interception from existing tool enforcement, while the gap between selected predicates and full policy prevents interpreting every extra block as an error. The supported contribution is a reproducible empirical audit connecting proposals, transitions and conversations. General effectiveness, unnecessary obstruction and superiority of decomposition remain unestablished.
